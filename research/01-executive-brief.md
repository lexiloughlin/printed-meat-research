# Executive research brief

## Business question
Can digitally structured meat alternatives earn enough repeat demand and contribution to cover production and commercial costs? The relevant test is a product/channel/geography combination, not whether a printer can manufacture an attractive prototype.

**Provisional analytical recommendation:** evaluate a narrow plant-based whole-cut foodservice pilot before committing to a dedicated manufacturing asset. This is an analyst hypothesis, not evidence of superior channel economics. Foodservice offers a testable unit (a buyer account with repeat orders), controlled preparation, and observable kitchen performance. A distributor or restaurant customer must validate those advantages.

## Economic logic

A printer only creates business value when customers pay for differentiated sensory or operational performance. Revenue equals the smaller of contracted demand and saleable capacity multiplied by net realized price. High theoretical output can worsen economics if it increases idle capacity and fixed costs. Gross sales are not revenue available to the manufacturer when distributor discounts, returns, rebates, or trade spending apply.

The model therefore separates installed capacity, uptime, production performance, first-pass quality, demand, net price, variable cost, fixed cost, capital, and cash timing. Scenario cases are coherent bundles of assumptions. The Monte Carlo output is a stress exploration of specified distributions, not a measured likelihood of commercial success.

## What would change the recommendation

Proceed from research to pilot only with a product legally marketable in the selected jurisdiction, a written supplier specification, a costed cold chain, and a buyer willing to test repeat orders. Proceed from pilot to scale only if measured contribution, repeat demand, food quality, and required throughput clear predeclared gates. Prefer contract production while demand remains uncertain if quotes show it reduces downside; do not assume outsourcing is cheaper.

A retail-first strategy may become preferable if buyer access, slotting economics, shelf life, and observed store velocity support it. Cultivated or hybrid products require a separate cost and regulatory model; replacing the ingredient cost in the plant-based case is insufficient.

## What the illustrative model actually shows

The base configuration sells 86,400 kg annually, produces $120,352 of steady-state operating contribution, and has a five-year project NPV of approximately −$1.54 million. This is an internal model result, not a conclusion about the industry. The ramp, fixed costs, and initial capital explain why positive mature operating contribution does not recover the modeled investment. The downside remains demand-constrained; the upside becomes capacity-constrained. The exact values and coherent input bundles are in `results/summary.md` and `data/assumptions.json`.

At the base demand level, higher machine availability alone adds no sales: demand already binds. This is why validating repeat orders can matter more than purchasing a faster machine. That implication follows from the illustrative constraint model and should be tested against real buyer data.
