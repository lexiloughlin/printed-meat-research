# Technology, value chain, and competition

## Segment by ingredient biology and structuring process

| Segment | Core process | Commercial question | Principal evidence gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant-protein whole cuts | Formulate printable pastes; deposit protein and fat; set and finish | Does structuring improve willingness to pay or repeat preference enough to cover conversion cost? | Matched sensory and cost comparison against extrusion, molding, and other texturization |
| Fungal / mycoprotein analogues | Ferment biomass; formulate and structure | Does fibrous biomass plus fat placement create differentiated products? | Fermentation economics and realized structuring throughput |
| Cultivated bioprinted tissue | Expand cells; assemble tissue; mature and finish, with process-specific ordering | Can cell production and tissue formation scale together? | Media cost, cell density, tissue maturation, yield, contamination, and process-specific safety |
| Hybrid products | Combine plant or fungal structure with cultivated components | Does a limited cultivated fraction add enough sensory value? | Ingredient economics and product-specific jurisdiction |

“Printed” describes a manufacturing operation; “cultivated” describes ingredient origin. Fungal ingredients should be classified explicitly rather than treated as botanically plant-derived. Not all cultivated products are printed, and not all structured whole cuts require 3D printing.

## Value chain and analytical data requirements

1. **Inputs:** protein, fat, binders, flavor, color, water; track delivered price, allergens, lead time, and lot variability.
2. **Preparation:** mixing, hydration, temperature and rheology control; measure batch time and out-of-spec losses.
3. **Structuring:** deposition speed, nozzle geometry, changeovers, cleaning; log scheduled and running time separately.
4. **Finishing:** setting, cooling, cooking where applicable; calculate mass balance and downstream bottlenecks.
5. **Release and distribution:** packaging, testing, cold storage, transport, expiry, returns.
6. **Consumption:** kitchen handling, cook loss, portion yield, satisfaction, and repeat orders.

Evaluate the whole line. A faster nozzle is not useful when chilling, packaging, quality release, or demand constrains production.

## Alternative business models

- **Branded food:** gross margin depends on realized price after channel deductions, customer acquisition, spoilage, and production cost.
- **Equipment plus consumables:** recurring margin depends on installed active machines and actual ink/premix usage, not machines shipped alone. Include warranty, installation, service, and concentration risk.
- **Technology licensing:** royalties require licensee commercial production; signed agreements and recognized recurring revenue are different events.
- **On-site service:** customer value may include customization and labor changes; account for kitchen footprint, cleaning, operator training, and downtime.

Compare business models on contribution and cash generation with consistent revenue definitions. Funding, patents, announced partnerships, and theoretical capacity are leading indicators, not substitutes for sales.

## Technical research protocol

For each paper record formulation, printer settings, sample dimensions, cooking method, comparator, mechanical tests, sensory panel, replicates, and limitations. Distinguish instrumental hardness from sensory preference and clinical/nutritional outcomes. The legacy soy-protein study [L01] should be rechecked in full before quoting its detailed parameter results; publisher access was blocked during this review.

Require a matched experiment: hold recipe and cooking constant when estimating the causal effect of printing geometry. When both change, attribute the result to the product system rather than the printer alone.
